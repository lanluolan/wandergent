# Travel Guidelines

Use live research for time-sensitive or location-specific facts. Do not rely on memory alone for unstable travel details.

## Research checklist

### Entry and legal requirements

Verify when relevant:

- visa / entry rules for the traveler's nationality
- passport validity requirements
- arrival forms or tourist taxes
- vaccination or health requirements
### Seasonal and date-sensitive context

Research:

- expected weather for the exact travel window
- peak / shoulder / low season
- major public holidays or festivals
- whether specific attractions are likely to sell out
- ticket prices or reservation fees for paid attractions in the draft itinerary
### Local operations

Check:

- opening hours
- closed days
- timed-entry requirements
- reservation urgency
- transportation schedules that affect the itinerary
- likely transit costs, passes, airport transfer costs, or other transport fees that materially affect the budget
### Accommodation research

When recommending a hotel, evaluate it against the user's stated priority:

- transit convenience
- atmosphere
- budget
- nightlife
- quiet / sleep quality

Explain why the chosen base fits the itinerary shape.
### Practical travel realities

Research:

- airport transfer options
- neighborhood recommendations
- scam / safety cautions
- local meal timing
- tipping and payment norms
- restaurant reservation pressure and typical meal cost levels when food stops matter to the itinerary
## Itinerary heuristics

- Cluster activities geographically.
- Protect arrival and departure days from overplanning.
- Put weather-sensitive activities in the best window of the day.
- For each outdoor-heavy day, think through a rain or low-energy backup.
- Put high-demand attractions where reservations and queue patterns make sense.
- Avoid making every day the same tempo.
- Distinguish free anchors from paid anchors so budget trimming is easy.
- Leave room for overruns, transit, and meal drift.
## Meal planning heuristics

Meals should match the route and the traveler's style.

Include when relevant:
- whether breakfast is realistic for the traveler's rhythm
- lunch placed near the actual route, not as filler
- whether dinner is a convenience stop or a destination meal
- whether reservations matter
- whether the user wants food highlights or just practical meal coverage
- likely cost level when a restaurant is a real anchor
- whether named restaurant recommendations are worth the added planning rigidity
## Budget heuristics

Provide a useful budget view, not fake precision.

Include:

- accommodation range
- food range
- transportation expectations
- airport transfer and local transit cost expectations
- activities / entry tickets
- contingency buffer

If prices are current and material, research them. Otherwise, present realistic ranges and say they are estimates.
For any ticketed stop you actively recommend, surface the likely entrance fee or clearly mark it as estimated before writing the plan into Wanderlog.
## Pre-Wanderlog review

Before executing the final Wanderlog write, quickly check:

- pace realism
- transit burden
- ticket-cost burden
- transport-cost burden
- weather backup coverage
- meal placement logic
- reservation / sellout risk

## Chat-first rule

Always present the plan in chat first. Use Wanderlog MCP only after the plan is coherent and the user wants it created or updated there.
