# Planning Criteria

Use these criteria to decide what questions to ask and how to optimize the trip. Ask only for missing criteria that materially affect the plan.

## Core criteria

- destination and date window
- trip length
- traveler count / companion type
- trip purpose
- total budget, daily budget, or hotel budget target
- budget behavior
- preference for mostly free stops vs worthwhile paid attractions
- must-do items

## Optimization criteria
### Priorities and tradeoffs

Ask what the trip should optimize for when tradeoffs happen:

- minimize transit time
- avoid crowds
- maximize landmarks
- maximize food experiences
- stay within budget
- keep the trip relaxed
- balance iconic sights with hidden gems
### Daily rhythm

Important for itinerary timing:

- early bird vs late starter
- preferred wake-up time
- late nights / nightlife interest
- slow mornings vs packed mornings
- tolerance for consecutive full days

Examples:

- A late starter should not get a forced 8 AM museum schedule every day.
- A nightlife-focused traveler may prefer lighter mornings and later dinners.
### Pace preference

Use pace as a structural input, not a decorative label:

- relaxed
- moderate
- packed

Translate pace into number of anchors per day, transit tolerance, and built-in buffer time.
### Seasonality sensitivity

Always consider:

- peak / shoulder / low season
- heat, rain, snow, humidity
- daylight hours
- holidays and festivals
- reservation pressure

Examples:

- In high summer, avoid outdoor queue-heavy attractions at midday if alternatives exist.
- In rainy season, add indoor backup options or swap logic.
### Mobility and energy

Ask when relevant:

- walking tolerance
- stairs / hills tolerance
- accessibility needs
- heat / cold tolerance
- comfort with public transit, rideshare, or driving

### Hotel selection

When hotel choice matters, ask what should drive the pick:

- transit convenience
- neighborhood atmosphere
- budget
- nightlife access
- quiet / sleep quality

A hotel recommendation should reflect the trip shape, not just a generic central location.
### Food behavior

Ask when food is part of the trip:

- how food-forward the trip should be
- dietary restrictions
- reservation-heavy dining okay or not
- coffee / brunch importance
- fine dining vs casual / street food
- willingness to travel for a meal
- whether meals should be highlights or just convenient and efficient
- whether the traveler wants named restaurant picks or just neighborhood-level meal guidance
- meal budget expectations when restaurant choice will materially affect the plan
### Logistics style

Ask if it changes the shape of the plan:

- one hotel vs multiple bases
- day trips yes / no
- minimize hotel changes
- structured bookings vs spontaneous exploration
## High-impact default questions

When the user provides only a simple trip request, start with the smallest useful set:
1. What are your dates and trip length?
2. What should this trip optimize for most?
3. What pace do you want: relaxed, moderate, or packed?
4. Are you an early starter or do you prefer late starts / late nights?
5. What is your actual budget shape: total budget, daily budget, or hotel target?
6. What is your budget style?
7. Do you want to keep attractions mostly free, use a balanced mix, or include a few worthwhile paid stops?
8. Do you want destination food highlights, practical meal coverage, or both?
9. Any must-do places, foods, or constraints?
## Budget framing

Ask for one of these when budget materially affects the plan:

- total trip budget
- daily spend target
- hotel budget per night
- attraction / dining splurge tolerance

Do not treat a style label alone as a full budget. "Balanced value" still needs a number when accommodation choice or ticketed attractions matter.

## Budget behavior options

Ask the user rather than assuming:

- cheapest viable
- balanced value
- comfort-first
- selective splurge
## Paid vs free planning

When the itinerary includes viewpoints, museums, decks, tours, or other ticketed stops, classify them as:

- free and easy to fit
- paid but core to the trip
- optional paid upgrade

This helps the user trim cost without losing the shape of the itinerary.

## Constraints to always check when relevant

- jet lag on arrival
- weather backup plans
- reservation lead times
- closed days / opening hours
- local meal timing
- neighborhood clustering
- fatigue after day trips
